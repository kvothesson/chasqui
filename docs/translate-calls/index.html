Live call translationHow to translate a Zoom, Meet or Teams call in real time

You speak your language and the other person hears a translated voice in theirs. They answer, and you hear it in yours through your headphones. Only you install chasqui: the other person joins the call as usual.

Where this stands. Two-way translation was tested end to end with ChatGPT Voice on Windows, in both directions. We have no report yet from a real Meet, Zoom or Teams call with a person on the other side. It uses the same Windows audio devices, but please try it before a call that matters, and tell us how it went.

What you need

  • Windows 10 or 11, and headphones. With open speakers, your mic picks up the translation and it loops.
  • chasqui and an OpenAI API key with access to gpt-realtime-translate.
  • VB-Audio Cable (free), which carries your translated voice into the call.
  • A way to capture what the other person says: Stereo Mix turned on in Windows, or a second cable such as Hi-Fi Cable. Without one, only your side gets translated.

Step by step

  1. Open chasqui. The first time, the setup window checks your key, the cable and the device switcher.
  2. Choose your language in the left box (I SPEAK) and theirs in the right box (THEY SPEAK).
  3. Pick Headset mode and press START. Wait for the light to say ON ROUTE.
  4. Join the call. The app has to use the Windows default mic: if it has a specific mic selected, change it to the default one or to CABLE Output.
  5. When you finish, press STOP or close the window. chasqui gives your Windows mic and output back.

Prefer not to have your Windows defaults touched? Meet mode leaves them alone: inside the call app you set the mic to CABLE Output and the speaker to your second cable.

If the other person hears nothing

Call apps can treat a synthesized voice as noise and filter it out. The turn shows up in chasqui's log, but the other side hears silence. Set noise suppression to Low or Off:

AppSetting
Google MeetSettings > Audio > Noise cancellation
Microsoft TeamsSettings > Devices > Noise suppression
ZoomSettings > Audio > Background noise suppression

What it costs

A call runs two translation streams, one per direction, so it costs $0.102 a minute: $3.06 for 30 minutes, $6.12 for an hour. OpenAI bills it to your own key. There is no subscription, and the window shows the running total while you talk.

What to expect

  • Delay: usually a second or two. We measured about 1.6 seconds from the original words to the translated voice, and the last words of a turn can come a little later.
  • Mistakes: the model sometimes answers a short phrase instead of translating it, so "Did you get there okay?" can come back as a reply. Longer sentences hold up better. The window shows both sides, so you can catch it.
  • Privacy: the audio of both people goes from your PC to OpenAI, with no chasqui server in between. Tell the other person you are using a translator.
  • Languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Mandarin, Arabic, Russian and Hindi.
